# D13. What belongs to the user?

An agent stops here and asks. Do not record D13 yourself until the user has answered, and quote their answer in the reason.

**Hand over:** `16_validate/juicebox/out_JBAT.hic` and `.assembly`, `16_validate/sv/sv_calls.tsv`, `16_validate/curation_targets.tsv` (you write this one, under the header stage 15 puts there), the work directory's `README.md` filled in from its template, and the reports.

**The user decides:**

- Which assembly to adopt, when a change trades contiguity against placed sequence or risk.
- **Whether to trial anything outside the process.** That means a different method or setting, a tool the guides do not name, or a fix specific to this assembly. Propose it here, after the run is complete, with the evidence for it. Do not try it first. Check [IMPROVING](../IMPROVING.md) before proposing: it lists what has been tried, what is ruled out and why. If the user agrees, build it in a second work directory and give `agentssembler compare`'s table, where each gain lies, and the weakest change with how to revert it alone. The tangle check is not such a trial: it is part of D16.
- How much risk to accept for completeness. Tails ([D16](D16_tails.md)) and chains behind a gap ([D11](D11_gap_closures.md)) are the clearest cases. On 13Q010 and WatsonsChoice the user chose to err toward minimising assembly risk, as on the first Busk build, which left chain0099 unplaced; on 13Q010 the exception was a graph-only telomeric path carrying 19 genes short of three copies, placed in both builds ([D07](D07_telomere_tips.md)). On the 2026 Busk rebuild the user had chain0099 placed (14 of 14 raw read tails, TTT infeasible on its tangle) and a homozygous path closing a chr4_h1 gap. On 292 the user accepted more risk for completeness: the Hi-C allele rescue and the gene-carrying tails.
- Whether medium-confidence SVs stay as assembled after inspection.
- **Unlocalized scaffolds are part of the assembly** (`16_validate/unloc/`, written at ploidy 2 only: on 13Q010, a triploid, the genes short on the scaffolds and complete in unplaced sequence stay in the unplaced file, 3 in the adopted build and 21 before the user placed the chr5_h2 start path; `unloc_purge` 0.2): hand them over with the scaffolds, and `unplaced_rest.fasta` in place of the unplaced file. Give the user compleasm with and without them (`compleasm/hK_unloc/`), the Mb by phase evidence (most are `arbitrary_*`: phase unknown), and the duplication cost; leaving them out, or another `unloc_purge`, is the user's call. They add genes without touching the scaffolds; they do not replace placing a chain when the evidence allows it. A chain at about 2x depth with no allele placed goes to both haplotypes (`copy_2x`), and its unloc fragments are purged against it.
- **Rescue swaps** (`13_scaffold/rescue_swaps.tsv`, stage 11): Hi-C gaps that would close if two chains swapped homologs, because the graph links the pair the other way from the rescue's Hi-C call. Neither side is proof in near-identical haplotypes, so the choice is the user's, asked at D16 where possible (a swap then skips a second build); any row left open comes here: keep the call, or swap them with a group edit and `reset 08_bridges`. On 292 the test found 2 of 46 Hi-C gaps, both at allele pairs the rescue had called by Hi-C. The build without the rescue had no gap at either site.
- With `hic_allele_rescue` on: the rescued regions for Juicebox review (orientation, and sequence on both haplotypes), from `10_groups/groups.rescued.tsv` and `sv/inv/merged.tsv`.
- Juicebox edits. **This route has not been exercised:** bring edits back as breaks, links or group edits with reasons, and re-run from the earliest affected stage. Do not paste a Juicebox export over the AGP.

**Declare this limit in any report:** the reference was used as an alarm during development. It shaped where people looked, so "reference-free" is true of the evidence behind each decision, not of the whole process.
